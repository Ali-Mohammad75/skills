# Content, links and pruning

## Content
Inspect the actual page and relevant search intent before recommending an update. Assess whether the page enables the user's decision: answer, explanation, credible evidence, specifications, price conditions, service coverage and useful next action. Review suitable competitors without copying or treating their statements as facts.

Use [content brief](../assets/content-brief.md) and the full [owner enrichment checklist](content-enrichment.md). Specify audience/context, primary task, target page, essential sections, evidence needed, useful media, CTA and internal links. Let format and length follow the need; do not force 300-word blocks, longer articles or every possible element. Separate draft copy from verified business/product claims.

Preserve successful useful content when updating; remove obsolete material where justified rather than always striking it through. Apply the owner’s separate editorial-comment/manual-rating workflow in [content enrichment](content-enrichment.md); do not turn synthetic work into observed user evidence or promise a freshness boost. For regulated or medical claims require appropriate primary evidence and qualified review before representing content as reviewed.

## Internal links
Inspect both source and destination, destination status/canonical relevance and existing links where available. Suggest an exact contextual placement and natural descriptive anchor. Identify existing versus proposed links and avoid duplicate recommendations. A source title alone supports only a candidate, not an exact insertion point.

Choose links for audience usefulness, semantic context and access to important pages. For outbound references, use the source site name or Naked URL, never this page’s main keyword. Do not link to domestic-market articles likely to compete with this page; relevant foreign other-language sources are allowed. These are owner preferences, not Google rules. There is no fixed keyword-density or link-count quota. Do not redirect all links to one page merely because its target keyword is valuable.

## Pruning
Build an inventory joining performance, index status, content, backlinks, conversions and business purpose where available. Pages such as contact, legal, support or new products may be useful without organic clicks.

Choose retain/update/merge/redirect/noindex/remove based on the page's actual role. A canonical is a preference for duplicates, not a substitute for every merge. A redirect needs a relevant destination; avoid mass homepage redirects. A noindex requires crawler access to be seen and is not a robots.txt crawl block. For each destructive change specify rationale, scope, dependencies, link/sitemap updates, rollback and monitoring. Prepare the plan before any newly required approval.
