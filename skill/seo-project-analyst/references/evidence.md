# Evidence and scope

Attach evidence to each material finding: source file or URL, relevant row/page/section, date, property, filters and the observed value. Mark evidence as observed, inferred, unverified or missing. Confidence belongs to the inference, not just the existence of data.

Use the actual source, not its title. A crawl export is a snapshot of what that crawler could access; a search export is not a complete inventory of pages. Absence from a limited or filtered export is not zero traffic. Read all relevant rows before making aggregate claims.

For a mismatch between course notes, a tool score and live evidence, describe the mismatch and verify the relevant official documentation. First-party search-engine policies establish eligibility/policy, not a guaranteed ranking formula. Third-party metrics such as DR and difficulty are proprietary estimates.

Keep a run record containing project/domain, task, analysis date, input filenames, data windows, scope exclusions, assumptions and output location. Use explicit dates and compare like-for-like time windows, country, device, search type and appearance filters. Note incomplete recent data and tracking changes.

Do not substitute the user's past projects for missing current-project facts. Ask only for information needed to make a consequential decision. Provide provisional recommendations with explicit conditions when useful.

When prioritizing, distinguish verified incidents from improvement opportunities. A verified sitewide indexing block may be urgent; an isolated excluded filter URL may be intentional. Do not count every tool warning as an error. Avoid universal scores presented as measured business impact.
